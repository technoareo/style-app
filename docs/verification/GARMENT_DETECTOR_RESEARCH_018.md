# Free multi-garment detector research (2026-10-22)

Perplexity was asked twice to find a free self-hosted detector with multiple garment instances, commercial-use permission, downloadable weights and CPU operation. The first answer proposed fine-tuning YOLO11/Detectron2 on DeepFashion2, which is not a ready-to-deploy pretrained detector and has license or data-rights questions. The follow-up named `resoa/garment-detector-seg` (Perplexity misspelled the account as `rcsoa` once). Root verified the actual [model card](https://huggingface.co/resoa/garment-detector-seg), [files](https://huggingface.co/resoa/garment-detector-seg/tree/main), [Fashionpedia data license](https://fashionpedia.github.io/home/data_license.html), and [RF-DETR documentation](https://github.com/roboflow/rf-detr/blob/develop/docs/index.md).

The model card lists Apache-2.0 weights, a 134 MB repository, RF-DETR-Seg-Small with 33.6M parameters, 46 classes (13 main garments plus accessories and parts), boxes/masks and confidence. Its published Fashionpedia validation reports main-garment box mAP 0.589 and overall mask mAP@[.5:.95] 0.352. The card explicitly warns that street/celebrity training photos do worse on flat-lay or hanger photos and that the published card lacks a measured failure rate/baseline comparison. These are author claims, not our measurements. RF-DETR docs describe CPU-compatible ONNX export, but this checkpoint is a PyTorch `.pth`, so no ready ONNX artifact or measured CPU latency for it has been found.

The official Fashionpedia license states that **annotations/ontology** are CC BY 4.0, but Fashionpedia does not own the **images**; use of those images follows source sites' terms. The model card's short 'Fashionpedia CC BY 4.0' line is therefore incomplete. The checkpoint's listed Apache license is useful evidence, but this research does not resolve all provenance/commercial rights. DeepFashion2's official repository did not yield a clear blanket commercial license in the earlier review. Ultralytics YOLO11's AGPL/Enterprise choice and the absence of garment-specific pretrained weights make it a poor drop-in for a private closed-source launch. Detectron2's Apache license covers its framework, not a ready garment checkpoint.

No detector or Ollama runtime is installed in this workspace. The model has not been downloaded, benchmarked, integrated, or compared with real user photos. Next valid step is an **isolated evaluation**, after confirming model provenance and acquiring representative consented photos: measure per-garment recall/precision, crop IoU, false garments from prints/background, and latency on the target host, stratified by flat-lay, hanger, worn, crowded and diverse garment types. Retain human review, the current six-item safety cap and photo-only flow until performance justifies a change. This is research, not a production-ready recommendation.
